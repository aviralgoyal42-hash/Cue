# Project Cue: Validation and Limitations

Status: covers Stages 1–7 and Experiments 1–6 (upstream model, SCI condition, synthetic sensor, CustomTechDevice, user trigger, hypothetical stimulator, safety controller, full loop and faults).

**Scope statement.** Project Cue is an early-stage research prototype for education and a competition demonstration. Every result here is a **model prediction**. Nothing in this repository shows clinical effectiveness or safety.

Supporting documents:
- `docs/STAGE1_REPOSITORY_REVIEW.md`: sources and code audit
- `docs/STAGE2_REPRODUCTION.md`: exact reproduction of the upstream model
- `docs/STAGE3_SCI_BASELINE.md`: SCI results

### Animal and human evidence
- **Animal:** the only experimental validation data are from rat cystometry (adult male Wistar rats; Jabbari & Erfanian 2019).
- **Human:** the only human evidence is literature *ranges*: intervoid interval, voiding duration, compliance and urine output. No human patient data were fitted.
- **SCI:** no SCI data of any kind were used, by the upstream authors or by us.

## 3. SOURCED values that are not validated, stress-tested for the SCI result

The paper says the neural parameters "lack empirically defined physiological ranges". So we checked whether the main SCI result depends on them. Each one was scaled at the 1000 mL ceiling under both drive rules.

| Upstream value | Scaling tried | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| `somatic_a` (sphincter drive strength, 0.5) | ×0.75, ×0.9, ×1.1, ×1.25 | **The result flips.** At ×0.9 and ×0.75 the urethra opens at about 485 and 468 mL, at 7.0 and 6.8 h. It then **leaks continuously for the rest of the day**: about 1,470–1,490 mL leaked, flow ≤ 0.07 mL/s, with volume held at about 470–490 mL and p_D at 45–55 cmH₂O. This is an overflow-type dribble, not a reflex void. At ×1.1 and ×1.25 there is no leak, the same as the default. |
| `parasympathetic_a` (detrusor excitation, 0.8) | ×0.75, ×1.25 | Peak p_D 45.3 and 57.3 cmH₂O. No leak. Even ×1.25 stays below the 59.1 cmH₂O closure pressure. |
| `sympathetic_a` (detrusor inhibition, 0.6) | ×0.75, ×1.25 | Peak p_D 56.9 and 56.3 cmH₂O. No leak. |

**Margin.** Leak or no leak depends on a margin of about 2.5 cmH₂O: a peak p_D of 56.6 against a sphincter closure pressure of 59.1. A 10% weaker sphincter drive, a parameter nobody has measured, is enough to change the outcome. This is a fragile prediction and has to be presented that way.

## 4. Which results are model predictions, not established facts

1. "With voiding disabled, the SCI bladder fills to whatever ceiling we set, with no leak and a pressure peak of about 56.6 cmH₂O near 500 mL." This is a **model prediction**, and it is fragile (§3).
2. "Pressure falls as the bladder over-fills." This is a **model prediction** from the over-stretch part of the force–length curve and from extrapolating the drives. Clinically, high storage pressure is a known SCI concern. **Do not present this as SCI behaviour.**
3. "With a weaker sphincter, continuous overflow dribbling occurs." This is a **model prediction**.
4. The model **cannot** show neurogenic detrusor overactivity or detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia. Neither is present in any run.
5. "With an 11% best-case sensor error, an alert at 350–450 mL warns a median of 39–132 min before the model's adverse event." This is a **model prediction** with a synthetic proxy sensor. The anchor comes from 5 healthy volunteers lying on their backs, not from people with SCI.
6. "No threshold works for all sensor faults, and misalignment defeats the alert." These are **model predictions** under ASSUMED fault sizes.
7. "Stimulation without sphincter co-activation empties the bladder in one session; with co-activation of 0.5 or more it does nothing." This is a **model prediction** that depends entirely on an ASSUMED set-up. It says nothing about real sacral stimulation.
8. "The pressure ceiling trades emptying against pressure." This is a **model prediction** with ASSUMED ceilings.
9. "The safety rules held in all 636 Experiment 3 and 4 runs." This is a fact **about the simulation code**, not about any real device.
10. "75 of 108 settings work in Experiment 3b." This is a **model prediction** from a POST-HOC, OPTIMISTIC experiment, with patterns chosen because they empty. It is not a recommendation.
11. "A 0.2–0.3 s pressure spike appears when flow stops." This is a **model prediction** traced in one seed. Whether real bladders do this is unknown.
12. "Cap 4 with drop 0.40 passes Experiment 7." This is a **model prediction** with ASSUMED values, a best-case sensor and thin margins. The rule was designed after seeing the fault, catheterisation is not modelled, and it is not a recommendation.
13. "The rule never blocked stimulation silently in 1,620 rule-on runs." This is a fact **about the simulation code**, not about any real device.
14. "No Experiment 3 or 4 verdict changed with 15 seeds, and the safety rules held in all 3,180 runs." The first part is a **model prediction** about inflow noise only (E95). The second is a fact **about the simulation code**.

**What is established outside this project** (from sources, not from our runs): SCI commonly brings neurogenic detrusor overactivity and detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia (de Groat & Yoshimura 2012); no prospective validation of wearable bladder ultrasound exists in adults with SCI (Kim & Kim 2026); the wearable patch study had 5 healthy volunteers (Toymus et al. 2024).
